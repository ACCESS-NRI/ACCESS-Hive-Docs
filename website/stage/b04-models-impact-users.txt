## What is the ESM 1.5{Q}

An ESM (Earth System Model) simulates the carbon and other bio-chemical cycles alongside climate. The ACCESS Earth System Model (ACCESS-ESM) is a fully-coupled global climate model that includes [atmosphere](https://docs.access-hive.org.au/models/model_components/atmosphere), [land](https://docs.access-hive.org.au/models/model_components/land), [ocean](https://docs.access-hive.org.au/models/model_components/ocean), [sea ice](https://docs.access-hive.org.au/models/model_components/sea-ice), [ocean biogeochemistry](https://docs.access-hive.org.au/models/model_components/bgc_ocean) and [land biogeochemistry](https://docs.access-hive.org.au/models/model_components/bgc_land) components, linked together by a [coupler](https://docs.access-hive.org.au/models/model_components/coupler).

![]({U}2024/09/ACCESS-ESM-Model-New-300x169.png)

ACCESS-ESM1.5 is one of the two ACCESS global coupled model versions. It includes land and ocean biogeochemistry.

The simulations of this ACCESS coupled model configuration provide extensive datasets encompassing a wide range of variables that characterise the climate, such as temperature, rainfall, cloud cover, sea-ice extent and ocean circulation. This model allows climate scientists to simulate climate to 2100 (and beyond) for a range of future socioeconomic pathways.

## How is ACCESS-ESM1.5 used{Q}

The last [Coupled Model lntercomparison Project, CMIP6,](https://wcrp-cmip.org/) used a wide range of simulations from the ACCESS-ESM1.5 version. This model allows climate scientists to simulate climate to 2100 (and beyond) for a range of future socioeconomic pathways.

ACCESS-NRI has released [ACCESS-ESM1.5 configurations](https://github.com/ACCESS-NRI/access-esm1.5-configs) as an adaptation of those originally developed by [CSIRO](https://www.csiro.au/en/research/environmental-impacts/climate-change/climate-science-centre) and [CLEX CMS](https://github.com/coecms/access-esm).

=====PATH /models/access-om/ | TITLE Ocean-sea ice Model (ACCESS-OM) | DESC ACCESS-OM2 is the ocean and sea-ice component of the ACCESS coupled model. | DATE
![]({U}2022/03/ACCESS-OM-Model.png)

ACCESS-OM2 is the ocean and sea-ice component of the ACCESS coupled model. ACCESS-OM2 provides the Australian climate community with ocean weather and climate research, including seasonal forecasting, climate variability studies, downscaling of climate in the marine environment around Australia, and ocean biogeochemistry modelling.

ACCESS-OM2 is an Earth System model configuration that links the Modular Ocean Model (MOM), the sea-ice model (CICE), a file-based atmosphere called YATM (Yet Another Atmospheric Model) and the Ocean-Biogeochemistry Model (WOMBAT), coupled together using the OASIS coupler.

ACCESS-OM2 has been developed by [Consortium for Ocean-Sea Ice Modelling in Australia (COSIMA)](http://cosima.org.au/) in collaboration with the [Geophysical Fluid Dynamics Laboratory](https://www.gfdl.noaa.gov/) (for an ocean model component), the Los Alamos National Laboratory (for a sea-ice model component), and the [European Centre Centre Européen de Recherche et de Formation Avancée en Calcul Scientifique (CERFACS)](https://cerfacs.fr/en/) (for an atmosphere-ocean OASIS3-MCT coupler).

=====PATH /models/access-ram/ | TITLE Regional Atmosphere Model (ACCESS-rAM) | DESC ACCESS-rAM3 is a fully supported regional model, created to understand and project weather with a unique Australian focus. | DATE
![]({U}2025/05/ACCESS-Regional-Model-2025-04-04-FINAL-300x167.jpg)

ACCESS-rAM3 is a fully supported regional model, created to understand and project weather with a unique Australian focus. This model includes atmosphere and land components, and it is an implementation of the UK Met Office (UKMO) regional nesting suite.

ACCESS-rAM3 allows Australian researchers to choose the time and location they wish to simulate. It also enables users to seamlessly use land-surface initial conditions from other available sources rather than relying on operational data, which are not readily available to the public.

This model enables the vital research undertaken by the [*ARC Centre of Excellence for 21st Century Weather*](https://www.21centuryweather.org.au/), aiming to understand how Australia’s weather will transform as our climate changes.

The rAM3 model is specially designed to run on the National Computing Infrastructure (NCI) platform available to the Australian research community.

The Australian focus of this release has been enhanced by a new functionality introduced by ACCESS-NRI, an improvement of the soil moisture initialisation using ERA5-Land from the [European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/forecasts) (ECMWF) and the BARRA reanalysis from the [Bureau of Meteorology](http://www.bom.gov.au/).

The future for the next versions of the ACCESS regional model will be to connect the Australian developed land-surface model, CABLE, as it better represents Australian vegetation and soil. Currently, ACCESS-rAM3 uses the UKMO land-surface model JULES.

=====PATH /eoi-model-evaluation-and-dev/ | TITLE Call for Expressions of Interest in Model Evaluation & Development | DESC Call for Expressions of Interest to work with ACCESS-NRI on testing and evaluation of ACCESS-ESM3 and its components. | DATE
Quick links: [This opportunity](#Opportunity) | [ACCESS-ESM3 Status](#ACCESS-ESM3) | [Forward Plan](#Plan) | [What to include in your EoI](#EoI) | [How EoIs will be evaluated](#Evaluation) | [Contact us](#Contact)

ACCESS-NRI recently received funding from the National Collaborative Research Infrastructure Strategy (NCRIS) to create Australia’s Next-Generation Earth System Model, ACCESS-ESM3.

## This Opportunity { #Opportunity }

We are calling for **Expressions of Interest (EoI)** from qualified research teams and individuals to work with ACCESS-NRI in testing and evaluation of ACCESS-ESM3 and its components. The ACCESS-ESM3 forward plan requires both technical development (primarily done by ACCESS-NRI) and improvements in model quality. Improving the quality of the model involves an iterative cycle of model testing and evaluation that will benefit from expertise in the research community.

ACCESS-NRI will support successful bids in one of three ways:

1. By providing funding to ACCESS-NRI partner organisations through subcontracts to employ staff to deliver key testing/evaluation milestones;
2. By temporarily seconding staff to ACCESS-NRI from partner organisations, or
3. By directly employing new staff on short (<12 months), fixed-term employment contracts at ACCESS-NRI.

## Current status of ACCESS-ESM3 { #ACCESS-ESM3 }

ACCESS-ESM3 is currently under development. The preliminary steps have been to focus on developing the component models, with a view to progressively assembling the full Earth system model:

- ACCESS-OM3 is the ocean-sea ice model component (MOM6-CICE6-NUOPC-WOMBAT). The 25km configuration is in the beta-release phase, and initial simulations are being evaluated. An 8km configuration is in the prototype phase and currently running on Gadi. Lower resolution (100km) configurations are used for testing and developing WWIII surface wave coupling.
- ACCESS-AM3 is the atmosphere-land model (UM13-CABLE3). 130km and 25km configurations have been beta released.
- CABLE3 and WOMBAT-lite have been developed for ACCESS-ESM1.6, involving substantial upgrades.
- ACCESS-CM3 is the coupled ocean-atmosphere model (merging OM3 and AM3). It is in the prototype phase and several 50-year simulations have been run (130km/25km resolution). Current efforts are focused on incorporating the CABLE3 land surface model.

## ACCESS-ESM3 Forward plan { #Plan }

Over the next 2 years planned development activities (and associated timelines) include:

- Full release configurations of ACCESS-OM3 at 25km and 8km resolution (2027);
- Full release configurations of ACCESS-AM3 at 130km and 25km resolution (2027);
- Develop high-resolution ACCESS-CM3 configuration at 25km/8km (and perhaps an intermediate-resolution option at 50km/25km) to provide the option of submission to HighResMIP within CMIP7 (by 2028);
- Develop ACCESS-ESM3 at standard resolution (130km/25km) in time for a potential Australian CMIP7 submission (by 2028);
- Upgrading CABLE3 to CABLE4, to include dynamic vegetation and fire impacts, and
- Providing options for WOMBAT-mid and WOMBAT-full implementation.

## How EoIs will be evaluated { #Evaluation }

A panel of ACCESS-NRI staff and Board members will be convened to determine the optimal deployment of funds. They will evaluate expressions of interest based on:

1. Value for money – the potential for the bid to make a substantial improvement in ACCESS-ESM3 performance.
2. The balance of development and evaluation work across the ACCESS-ESM3 component models.
3. The track record and capability of applicants.
4. The extent to which outputs will be openly shared with the community through evaluation methods supported by ACCESS-NRI.
5. The ability of funded staff to focus on ACCESS evaluation and development (> half-time commitments strongly preferred).
